# Cute design rules

House rules for making a pet cute. Written by Fable (as Astra) on 2026-11-19, after Spencer called the Kitten blockout "too ugly". Use this with `PET-STYLE-GUIDE.md`: that page says what a pet is made of, this page says what makes it cute. Where they disagree on a number, flag it; section 6 lists the three places they do.

Every rule has a number a modeller can measure and a one-line source. The research gives the **direction** (bigger eyes, smaller nose, rounder shapes). The **exact numbers** are our house choice, set from the Bear's real measurements and checked on the Bunny blockout. They are marked "house number".

Sheets: `artifacts/v2-pet-redesign-2026-11-19/` (`03-bunny-face-measured.png` shows the face rules drawn on a head).

## How to measure

- **Head height** = chin to crown, ears not counted. **Head width** = widest point of the head.
- **Eye** = the dark part you actually see. A white ring does not count as eye.
- **Eye line** = height of the eye centres, as a percentage up the head from the chin.
- All sizes are checked in a straight front view.

## 1. Proportion

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | The head is **50 to 60%** of the height from feet to crown (about 2 heads tall). | Chibi guides put super-deformed characters at 2 to 3 heads, with the head a third to a half of the height: [Clip Studio chibi ratio](https://tips.clip-studio.com/es-es/articles/4806?org=1). Gould measured Mickey's head growing from 42.7% to 48.1% of body length as he got cuter: [re-measurement](https://cognitionandculture.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/621/index.html). |
| P2 | The head is **1.5 to 1.7 times** as wide as the body. Never a 50/50 split between head and body. | "Aim for 60/40, avoid 50/50, the parts compete": [Clip Studio, Analysis of a Cute Creature](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7185). |
| P3 | The head is **wider than tall** (width 1.2 to 1.35 times height), with the widest point at or below the eye line (round cheeks). | A wider, rounder face is one of the six features that raised cuteness ratings: [Glocker et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/). |
| P4 | Arms are **under 50%** of head height. Legs are feet only. **No neck.** | Short, thick limbs and a plump body are part of the baby schema as summarised in [Kringelbach et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4956347). House number. |
| P5 | Long parts (ears, tails) are allowed only if they are **thick**: width at least 22% of length. Thin and long reads goofy or sickly. | "Long tails or necks can look goofy; thin builds can look sickly": [Clip Studio](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7185). House number. |

## 2. Eyes

Children spend about 46% of their looking time on the eyes of a face, human or animal ([Borgi et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4019884/)). The eyes are where most of the cuteness budget goes.

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Each eye (dark part) is **14 to 18%** of head width, and taller than wide (height 1.2 to 1.35 times width). | Enlarging the eyes alone raised cuteness in every expression and age group: [Yao et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8786738). Baby faces sit at about 18.5% eye width to face width: [Glocker 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/). |
| E2 | The eye line is **38 to 46%** up the head. Never above the middle. | A higher forehead raised cuteness: [Glocker 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/). Toy makers put eyes halfway down or lower: [Liz Haywood, On Designing Toy Faces](https://lizhaywood.com.au/?p=10983). |
| E3 | Eye centres are **44 to 50%** of head width apart, leaving a gap of 1.6 to 2 eye widths. | "Lower on the face and spaced further apart" for a cuter toy: [Shiny Happy World](https://www.shinyhappyworld.com/?p=23977); wider-set reads cuter: [Liz Haywood](https://lizhaywood.com.au/?p=10983). House number. |
| E4 | **No white around the eye on a coloured body.** The eye is one dark shape. White showing all round a pupil is how a startled face looks. | House rule from our own renders (`04-bear-tweaks.png`); "solid eyes are simpler; more detail raises the risk of creepy": [Liz Haywood](https://lizhaywood.com.au/?p=10983). |
| E5 | **Two catchlights** per eye: a big one (about 19% of eye width) upper left, a small one (about 8%) lower right. Same side on both eyes; never mirrored. | A studio convention, not a finding: I found no study on catchlights. House rule, tested on the blockout. |
| E6 | The eye is a warm near-black (`#2B1A20`), never pure black, and matte enough that real highlights do not fight the catchlights. | House rule. Dark features are the safe choice on light fabric: [Liz Haywood](https://lizhaywood.com.au/?p=10983). |
| E7 | Left and right eyes match within **0.01 studs** in height and size. | Small shifts change a toy's whole face: [Shiny Happy World](https://www.shinyhappyworld.com/?p=23977). House number. |

## 3. Face

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Every feature sits in the **lower 55%** of the head. The top 45% is clear forehead. | High forehead: [Glocker 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/). |
| F2 | The nose is **7% of head width or less**. It sits just under the eye line, between the eyes. | Smaller nose length and width raised cuteness: [Glocker 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/). |
| F3 | The mouth is **10% of head width or less**, right under the nose, and curves up at the ends. No teeth. | Smaller mouth raised cuteness: [Glocker 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/). "Wide mouths can look creepy; leave out teeth": [Liz Haywood](https://lizhaywood.com.au/?p=10983). |
| F4 | A muzzle or snout pad is **25% of head width or less** and sticks out no more than 0.05 studs. A big muzzle reads as an adult animal. | Short, small nose region: [Glocker 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/). Sugimori kept faces soft by not making "the eyes sharper and the head smaller": [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/07/ken_sugimori_wants_pokemon_designs_to_be_as_memorable_as_possible). House number. |
| F5 | **Blush:** two soft ovals, each about 16% of head width, outside and just below the eyes. | "Small blush spots are fine" on an otherwise minimal face: [Tuts+ kawaii tips](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/10-top-tips-for-creating-cute-kawaii-art--cms-25705). House number. |
| F6 | Count the face parts: eyes, nose, mouth, blush, one pad. **Nothing else.** No whiskers, brows, lashes or fur tufts by default. | "Simplicity is key; too much detail turns cute into creepy": [Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/10-top-tips-for-creating-cute-kawaii-art--cms-25705). |

## 4. Shape and silhouette

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **No silhouette corner sharper than 25 degrees** on the head and body. In practice: at least 16 segments round the head. Smooth shading alone does not hide a 10-sided outline. | People like curved objects more than sharp ones, and sharp corners read as threat: [Bar and Neta 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16913943/). |
| S2 | **No points.** Every tip (ear, tail, tuft) ends in a round cap with a radius of at least 0.04 studs. | Same source; "round off sharp points and corners so it looks soft and huggable": [Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/10-top-tips-for-creating-cute-kawaii-art--cms-25705). |
| S3 | Build from **balls, eggs and capsules** only. No cones, no boxes. | "Circles first; squares suggest harsh materials": [Clip Studio](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7185). Kirby started as a plain circle: [Kirby, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirby_(character)). |
| S4 | The pet must be named from its **black silhouette at 96 px tall**, from the front and the back. One feature does that job (for example the ears). | "Kawaii characters are minimal, so the silhouette has to tell them apart": [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/character-design/10-tips-kawaii-character-design-514833). |
| S5 | **One species feature, one charm point.** At most 3 new shapes on top of the shared body. Pikachu's team kept one idea per part and were told "cuter" on each pass. | [Pokemon.com, the creators of Pikachu](https://www.pokemon.com/uk/pokemon-news/creator-profile-the-creators-of-pikachu). |
| S6 | Allow **one small imperfection** (a flopped ear, a tilted tuft). Perfect symmetry everywhere reads as stiff. Never the eyes (E7). | Game Freak add a flaw to designs that are "too cute" or "too cool": [SoraNews24 on Sugimori](https://soranews24.com/2018/08/03/is-the-beauty-of-imperfection-the-secret-behind-the-popularity-of-pokemon-character-designs/amp/). |
| S7 | Highlights must not show facets. Big forms use roughness **0.45 to 0.55**; if a highlight still breaks into flat patches, add geometry. | House rule from the Kitten renders. |

## 5. Colour

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Starting body colour: HSV **saturation 15 to 35%, value 90% or more**. A pastel. | Brighter colours were picked as more kawaii for every hue: [Ohkura et al., kawaii colours](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-21793-7_12) (read from the abstract only). The saturation cap is a house number: the same study favours more saturation, but on a body this size 58% read as loud in our render. |
| C2 | Warm hues for a starter (pink, peach, cream, butter). Cool hues are for dyes. | "Pinks and yellows read loving and happy; blues and purples read grumpy": [Clip Studio](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7185). |
| C3 | The secondary is **lighter** than the primary and close in hue (a cream, not a contrast colour). | Soft gradients, not hard contrast: [Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/10-top-tips-for-creating-cute-kawaii-art--cms-25705). |
| C4 | At most **4 colour families** on a pet: primary, secondary, pink (nose and blush), eye dark. | "Limit to two, three or four colours": [Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/10-top-tips-for-creating-cute-kawaii-art--cms-25705). |
| C5 | Nothing on the pet is pure white except the catchlights. Nothing is pure black. | House rule: it keeps the catchlights the brightest thing on the face. |

## 6. Motion

Cuteness is not only a still picture; it also pulls on careful, gentle behaviour ([Nittono et al. 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0046362)) and it includes sound and movement ([Kringelbach et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4956347)). Timings are house numbers unless a source is given.

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Breathing squash:** height +3%, width and depth -1.5%, once every 2.4 s. Volume stays the same. | Squash and stretch keeps volume constant: [Twelve basic principles of animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation). |
| M2 | **Idle bob:** 0.03 to 0.05 studs, eased in and out, never linear. | Slow in and slow out: same source. |
| M3 | **Blink:** every 2.5 to 5 s at random. Close 2 frames, hold 1, open 3 (at 30 fps, 0.2 s). One blink in five is a double. | Frame counts: [Bloop Animation](https://www.bloopanimation.com/blinking-animation/). |
| M4 | **Always blink on a head turn.** | "A head turn would almost always get us to blink": [Bloop Animation](https://www.bloopanimation.com/blinking-animation/). |
| M5 | **Soft parts lag.** Ears and tail follow the head 0.10 to 0.15 s late and overshoot 10 to 15 degrees. | Follow through and overlapping action: [Twelve basic principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation). |
| M6 | **Squash before a hop:** 10% down for 0.1 s, stretch 8% in the air, squash 12% on landing. | Anticipation, squash and stretch: same source. |
| M7 | A little clumsy: a small wobble on landing, a head tilt of 8 to 12 degrees when curious. | Lorenz's baby schema is usually said to include clumsy movement; I did not check the original, so treat this as a house rule. |

## 7. Where this differs from PET-STYLE-GUIDE.md

1. **Eyes.** The style guide says "tall ovals with a white ring". E4 says no ring on a coloured body. Needs Spencer's call (see Bear tweaks).
2. **Triangles.** The style guide targets 1,200 to 1,800. S1 needs a rounder head, which costs about 900 more. The 4,000 ceiling still holds. Spencer chose the low-poly Dreamcast look on 2026-11-12, so S1 is the rule most likely to be overruled; every other rule works at the current budget.
3. **Gloss.** The style guide says roughness about 0.35. S7 asks for 0.45 to 0.55 on big forms.

### Why the Kitten read as ugly

Spencer's suspects were all real. In order of damage:

1. **Seven sharp points.** Cone ears, four cone cheek tufts and a thin tapering tail. Sharp reads as threat, and four spikes beside the face read as a mane or a lizard frill, not fluff.
2. **The white eye ring on orange.** On the white Bear the ring disappears and you see a dark oval. On orange you see white all round a small pupil: a startled stare. The dark part of the eye is only 11% of head width.
3. **A faceted, reshaped head.** I widened the cheeks and lowered the crown on a 150-face head. That flattened it, pushed the eye line up to 54%, and the glossy highlight broke into flat patches.
4. **Lumpy extras.** Two whisker pads, a nose and four tufts crowd the lower face. Eleven face parts where five do the job.
5. **Orange at 58% saturation,** twice the pastel range, next to near-white socks. Loud, not soft.

The Kitten was also built by adding parts. Cute comes from taking parts away.

### The Bear, honestly

The Bear passes the big proportion rules, which is why it already reads as cute. It misses on the face: eyes above the middle, a small dark pupil inside a ring, a muzzle a third of the head wide, no blush, and a ten-sided head outline. `04-bear-tweaks.png` shows a copy with those four things fixed. Nothing live was changed.
