## 2. Move more to eggs

Each new egg costs an odds panel, a fixed-next path for restricted accounts, a receipt state machine, specs and localization. Because Robux Star packs exist, every Star egg is paid random.

| Catalog | All direct | All egg | Hybrid | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wardrobe** (23 + 10) | Clear, fair, already built. No surprise. | A 33-item pool: players cannot aim for the hat they want, which is the worst frustration in a dress-up game. Restricted accounts get a fixed order, a worse shop. | Bases direct. **Recolours egg-only** in a no-duplicate "Dye Egg" (the Toy Egg model: uniform over what you do not own, never a dupe). | **Hybrid, v1.1.** Dye Egg 30 Stars, 10 recolours = 300 Stars, plus 4 to 6 new recolours per update at near-zero art cost. |
| **Faceplates** (10 sold) | The plate retints the whole UI; players want a specific one. | Low upside, same aim problem. | Later: palette variants (Grape Soda, Rose Chrome) could join the Dye Egg. | **Direct.** |
| **Ball themes** (5 sold) | They change round readability, a deliberate choice. | Pool too small to be fun. | None worth building. | **Direct.** |

So the answer to "more in eggs" is: yes for variants and looks (Dye Egg, Look Egg), no for base items. Gamification comes from no-duplicate pools and staged jars, not from hiding the base shop.

## 3. Look Egg: hues and finishes in one egg

**Possible? Yes, with one constraint.** Finishes are `SurfaceAppearance` templates built on flat roughness maps (82, 64, 36 of 255), not sliders. Stages are therefore more flat maps and templates, generated by the existing tooling (no AI art). Free blending like hue colour is not possible. What mixes meaningfully is two axes:

- **Sheen**: one 7-stop axis. Matte jars push left (3 stops), Gloss jars push right (3 stops). They cancel, so they share a slider.
- **Jelly**: 3 stages of translucency, scaling the existing per-part `JellyAlphaByPart` (a runtime number, cheap).
- **The mix**: Sheen x Jelly. Glossy Jelly, Frosted (matte) Jelly and so on: 28 looks from 3 jars. Metal, Pearl Edge and Speckle Satin can join later as further axes.

**Save.** Additive only. Nothing is removed or reinterpreted. Because the validators check exact keys, treat it as a v12 step in the same style as v10 `petWearables`, unless the architect confirms v11 tolerates new optional fields.

```
finishInventory = { gloss=n, matte=n, jelly=n }          -- jars held, like hueInventory
petFinish[petId] = { gloss=0..3, matte=0..3, jelly=0..3 } -- sheen = gloss - matte
lookReceipts / lookReceiptOrder / lookReservation         -- one new receipt family
lookMisses = 0..15                                        -- Jelly pity counter
```

**Migration.** `hueInventory`, `petHues` and all Hue and Material receipts stay untouched and valid; pools 1 to 4 stay frozen. `materialUnlocks` is read forever: an unlocked finish means free, unlimited doses of that finish on every pet, so nobody is worse off. The Hue Egg and Material Egg stop selling (`EnablePurchases=false`, Hue buy refused). If the launch save wipe stands and the Material Egg is switched off for v1 (see §4), no live player ever holds a permanent finish and the migration is nothing.

**Customize flow.** Hue and Finish tabs become one **Look** tab. Top: Main / Secondary toggle, four fruit jars with counts and five pips each (unchanged). Below: the Sheen slider with Matte and Gloss jars at its ends, and a Jelly jar with three pips. A tap spends one jar and the pet updates live. "Original" stays free per section. New jars show a badge on the card that clears on first tap, replacing the hatch result screen (Spencer's note).

**Odds and disclosure** (whole percentages, shown before buying):

| Rose | Tide | Citrus | Iris | Gloss | Matte | Jelly |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18 | 18 | 18 | 18 | 11 | 11 | 6 |

Pity, also disclosed: a Jelly jar is guaranteed if 15 eggs pass without one. Restricted or unknown-policy accounts: no roll, the jar they hold fewest of in the published order, named on the button. A full stack refuses without charging. At 12 Stars, a fully kitted pet (two hue channels, Gloss 3, Jelly 3) is about 45 to 50 eggs, **540 to 600 Stars**: about 4 engaged days or 13 casual play days, per pet, repeatable. Today all finishes cost 48 in total.

**Risks.**
1. Three roughness stops may not read on a small pet on a phone. Fable must approve renders first; fall back to 2 stages.
2. Each of about 14 templates (7 sheen levels, one-tone and two-tone) must pass the `NativeVerified` gate.
3. Consumable finishes replace permanent ones. The grandfather rule covers it, but only if it ships before many players own finishes.
4. Seven odds rows plus the pity line on a phone panel.
5. A second economy change days before launch.

**Build estimate.** Catalog, state, receipts, migration, specs: 2 Opus sessions. Look tab: 1 to 2. Four flat maps, templates, native verification and Fable render review: 1. Odds panel, restricted path, localization: 0.5. **About 5 to 6 sessions, 3 to 4 calendar days with review.**
